Contract — `split_claims(text: str) -> list[str]`:
- Deterministic sentence segmentation: split on `.` `!` `?` **and their CJK/full-width counterparts `。` `！` `？`** (plan-review M-7 — corpus questions and generated text may be CJK; the ASCII-only rule yields one giant claim and zero grounded citations) followed by whitespace or end-of-string; terminators stay with their sentence.
- `[n]` citation markers stay attached to the sentence they follow (segmentation must not orphan a marker into the next claim — T-023's grounding counts markers per claim).
- Common abbreviations best-effort protected by a small FIXED list (e.g. "Inc.", "No.", "U.S.", "Corp.", "Co."): a period inside a listed abbreviation does not split. The list is a module constant — additions are contract changes, not drive-by edits.
- Never returns empty/whitespace-only strings; leading/trailing whitespace trimmed per claim; text with no terminator yields exactly one claim; empty/whitespace-only input yields `[]`.
- The EXACT rule is whatever the frozen tests pin — keep it simple and documented in the module docstring; T-023 and T-026 import it and MUST NOT reimplement segmentation.

## Acceptance Criteria
- **AC-1**: Pinned cases — a 3-sentence paragraph with `[1]`/`[2]` markers segments into 3 claims with each marker attached to its own sentence; `!` and `?` terminators split; trailing-no-punctuation text yields one claim; empty string yields `[]`.
- **AC-2**: Abbreviation non-splits — "Dominion Energy Inc. requested 240 MW." is ONE claim; every entry in the fixed abbreviation list is exercised at least once collectively (parametrized).
- **AC-3**: CJK/full-width — text terminated by `。` / `！` / `？` segments at those terminators (a two-sentence `。`-separated fixture yields 2 claims), and a mixed ASCII+CJK paragraph segments at both terminator families (plan-review M-7).
- **AC-4**: Property (hypothesis) — for generated texts over a safe alphabet: rejoining the claims preserves all non-whitespace characters of the input, no claim is empty, and every `[n]` substring in the input appears in exactly one claim.
